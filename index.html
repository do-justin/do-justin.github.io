I am a fourth-year medical student at Thomas Jefferson University, currently applying into anesthesiology. At Jefferson, I am a research fellow in the Department of Anesthesiology and Perioperative Medicine and a research intern at the Health Design Lab.

My research focuses on the value of multimodal data for clinical prediction. I study whether intraoperative time series and physiologic waveforms add information beyond tabular clinical variables, and how we can interpret the models that use them. I hope to combine these signals with text, images, and audio.

I also study the emerging multimodal capabilities of large language models in medicine, with projects evaluating conversational image segmentation and home fall-risk assessment.

    Do J, Shah KH, Xu MC, Kim AH, Suresh V, Guggilla N, Li M, Kothari R.

    JAMIA Open. Jun 20, 2026;9(3):ooag092.

    Research article · Published · 0 Crossref citations (Nov 7, 2026)

    Paper: Integration of intraoperative data in interpretable machine learning models to predict postoperative AKI in noncardiac surgery patientsPubMed: Integration of intraoperative data in interpretable machine learning models to predict postoperative AKI in noncardiac surgery patientsBibTeX: Integration of intraoperative data in interpretable machine learning models to predict postoperative AKI in noncardiac surgery patients
    Summary: Integration of intraoperative data in interpretable machine learning models to predict postoperative AKI in noncardiac surgery patients

    This retrospective INSPIRE study compared preoperative models with models incorporating low-frequency intraoperative data for stage 2 or 3 postoperative acute kidney injury. Intraoperative summaries added limited discrimination, and a temporal deep-learning model did not outperform simpler tabular methods. The findings motivate testing whether higher-resolution physiologic signals add information beyond the preoperative record.

  1. Evaluating conversational image segmentation for medicine: performance, failure modes, and a fairness audit across seven modalities

    Do J, Guggilla N, Suresh V, Kothari R.

    medRxiv. Nov 23, 2025.

    Preprint · 0 Crossref citations (Nov 7, 2026)

    Preprint: Evaluating conversational image segmentation for medicine: performance, failure modes, and a fairness audit across seven modalitiesBibTeX: Evaluating conversational image segmentation for medicine: performance, failure modes, and a fairness audit across seven modalities
    Summary: Evaluating conversational image segmentation for medicine: performance, failure modes, and a fairness audit across seven modalities

    This preprint evaluates conversational segmentation across seven medical-imaging modalities and audits dermoscopy performance by skin-tone group. Performance varied substantially by modality, with weaker results on some radiologic targets. Differences in mask generation and overlap across the audited skin-tone groups show why aggregate benchmark performance can conceal important failure modes.

    Summary: Leveraging multimodal large language models for fall risk reduction in older adults in the home: proposed model design

    This research letter develops and evaluates a multimodal workflow for identifying home fall hazards and visualizing suggested modifications. A formative rating study compared baseline outputs with an enhanced, evidence-grounded pipeline. The findings support further design evaluation while preserving concerns about implausible or hazardous edits; clinical implementation and effects on falls remain untested.
